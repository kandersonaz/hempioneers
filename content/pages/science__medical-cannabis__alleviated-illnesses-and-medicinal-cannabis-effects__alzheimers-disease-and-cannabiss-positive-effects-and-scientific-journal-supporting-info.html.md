<div class="editortext">Alzheimer&#8217;s disease and Cannabis’s positive effects and Scientific Journal Supporting Info</div>
<div class="editortext"></div>
<div class="editortext">Lisa M. Eubanks, PhD, Staff Scientist at the Skaggs Institute for Chemical Biology, et al and the Scripps Research Institute stated in an Aug 2006 article titled &#8220;A Molecular Link Between the Active Component of Marijuana and Alzheimer&#8217;s Disease Pathology,&#8221; published in <i>Molecular Pharmaceutics</i>:</div>
<blockquote>
<div class="editortext">
<p align="justify">&#8220;In contrast to previous studies aimed at utilizing cannabinoids in Alzheimer&#8217;s disease therapy, our results provide a mechanism whereby the THC molecule can directly impact Alzheimer&#8217;s disease pathology&#8230;</p>
<p align="justify">It is noteworthy that THC is a considerably more effective inhibitor&#8230; than the approved drugs for Alzheimer&#8217;s disease treatment, donepezil and tacrine, which reduced [protein deposits in the brain] by only 22% and 7%, respectively, at twice the concentration used in our studies&#8230;</p>
<p>THC and its analogues may provide an improved therapeutic for Alzheimer&#8217;s disease [by] simultaneously treating both the symptoms and progression of Alzheimer&#8217;s disease.&#8221;</p>
</div>
<p>Aug. 9, 2006 &#8211; <a href="http://medicalmarijuana.procon.org/sourcefiles/Eubanks.pdf" target="_blank">Source Info Click Here</a></p></blockquote>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<div class="editortext">Maria L. de Ceballos, PhD, Group Leader in the Department of Neural Plasticity at the Cajal Institute in Spain, et al., wrote in their Feb. 23, 2005 <i>Journal of Neuroscience</i> article titled &#8220;Prevention of Alzheimer&#8217;s disease Pathology by Cannabinoids: Neuroprotection Mediated by Blockage of Microglial Activation&#8221;:</div>
<blockquote>
<div class="editortext">&#8220;Our results indicate that cannabinoid receptors are important in the pathology of AD [Alzheimer&#8217;s disease] and that cannabinoids succeed in preventing the neurodegenerative process occurring in the disease.</div>
</blockquote>
