<div class="editortext">Simon Lal, MD, PhD, Gastroenterologist at Spire Manchester Hospital and Spire Manchester Clinic Hale (England), et al. stated the following in their Oct. 23, 2011<i>European Journal of Gastroenterology and Hepatology</i>article, &#8220;Cannabis Use amongst Patients with Inflammatory Bowel Disease&#8221;:</div>
<blockquote>
<div class="editortext">&#8220;Patients with UC [ulcerative colitis], in particular, reported using cannabis to improve diarrhoeal symptoms, and, again, this perceived benefit has received mechanistic support from studies that suggest that cannabinoids inhibit intestinal secretory responses&#8230; It is equally plausible to speculate that patients perceived benefit from using cannabis to reduce pain and/or diarrhoea because the drug has a direct anti-inflammatory effect on intestinal tissue… As in other diseases, a significant proportion of both UC and CD patients reported using cannabis to enhance appetite, and the drug&#8217;s orexigenic properties are well-recognized, with many individuals commonly reporting appetite stimulation or &#8216;the munchies&#8217; after use.&#8221;</div>

<div class="editortext">Timna Naftali, MD, Specialist in Gastroenterology at Meir Hospital and Kupat Holim Clinic (Israel), et al., stated the following in their Aug. 2011 <i>Israel Medical Association Journal</i> article titled &#8220;Treatment of Crohn’s Disease with Cannabis: An Observational Study&#8221;:</div>
<blockquote>
<div class="editortext">
<p>&#8220;Of the 30 patients [with Crohn&#8217;s Disease] 21 improved significantly after treatment with cannabis…</p>
<p>The mean number of bowel movements decreased from eight to five a day and the need for other drugs was significantly reduced… the number of patients requiring steroid treatment was reduced from 26 to 4. Fifteen of the patients had 19 surgeries during an average period of 9 years before cannabis use, but only 2 required surgery during an average period of 3 years of cannabis use…</p>
<p>The observed beneficial effect in this study may be due to the anti-inflammatory properties of cannabis, but additional effects of cannabinoids may also play a role. Cannabinoids influence gastrointestinal motility and, in particular, have an anti-diarrheal effect&#8230;&#8221;</p>
