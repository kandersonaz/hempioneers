<p>HIV AIDS Medicinal Cannabis</p>
<ol>
<li>The <i>Annals of Internal Medicine</i>, stated in its Aug. 19, 2003 article titled &#8220;Short-Term Effects of Cannabinoide in Patients with HIV-1 Infection&#8221;:</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<div class="editortext">
<p align="justify">&#8220;Conclusions: Smoked and oral cannabinoids [marijuana] did not seem to be unsafe in people with HIV infection with respect to HIV RNA levels, CD4 and CD8 cell counts, or protease inhibitor levels over a 21-day treatment.&#8221;</p>
<p align="justify">&#8220;Patients receiving cannabinoids [smoked marijuana and marijuana pills] had improved immune function compared with those receiving placebo.  They also gained about 4 pounds more on average than those patients receiving placebo.&#8221;</p>
</div>
<p>Aug. 19, 2003 <a href="http://medicalmarijuana.procon.org/view.source.php?sourceID=000229">Click Here for Source</a></p></blockquote>
<p>2. Lynn Zimmer, PhD, wrote in her 1997 book <i>Marijuana Myths, Marijuana Facts: A Review of the Scientific        Evidence</i>:</p>
<blockquote>
<div class="editortext">
<p align="justify">&#8220;There is no evidence that marijuana users are more susceptible to infections than non-users.</p>
<p align="justify">Early studies that showed decreased immune function in cells taken from marijuana users have since been disproven.</p>
<p align="justify">Indeed, not a single case of marijuana-induced immune impairment has ever been observed in humans.&#8221;</p>
</div>
<p>1997 &#8211; <a href="http://medicalmarijuana.procon.org/view.source.php?sourceID=000233">Click Here for Source</a></p></blockquote>
